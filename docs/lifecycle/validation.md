# Demand validation · P0

Test whether a specific user has a painful job before committing to a build.
Use desk research, existing evidence, a bounded prototype or an automated test to
choose the next step. Participant testing belongs to the pre-production MVP
testing gate, after an MVP candidate exists.

## Checklist and proof

- [ ] Fill the [validation brief](validation-brief.md) for one user segment.
  Proof: the issue names the job, current workaround, and questions the available evidence must answer.
- [ ] List the riskiest demand assumption separately from a solution preference.
  Proof: one falsifiable hypothesis with a measurable outcome and deadline.
- [ ] Use non-participant evidence for the pre-MVP decision: desk research, existing
  support or usage evidence, a bounded prototype, or an automated test.
  Proof: sources, assumptions and observed behavior are recorded in the issue.
- [ ] Keep the decision provisional when participant evidence is still needed.
  Proof: the unresolved assumption names the pre-production MVP test and threshold.
- [ ] Compare the available evidence with the brief's success and stop criteria.
  Proof: the issue records proceed, revise, or stop with supporting observations.
- [ ] Carry unresolved assumptions into the spec as explicit tests.
  Proof: each remaining assumption has an owner, measure, and review date.

## Founder handoff

Apply the [authority rule](README.md#evidence-and-authority). Bring the founder
the evidence and recommendation before expanding the appetite. Defer participant
recruitment to MVP testing; do not make early design or scope decisions depend on
an unavailable sample. Keep any later participant details in an approved private
location and link only redacted summaries.

## Exit

- [ ] Founder records the demand decision and next experiment or spec issue.
  Proof: link to the decision; interest alone does not establish willingness to act.
- [ ] Schedule when to revisit the decision after observing real use.
  Proof: an issue has a date and the metric that would change the decision.
